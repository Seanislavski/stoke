# Running a Community on Stoke — Organizer Guide

Welcome, organizer. This guide will take you from an empty community to a thriving one. It's split into two parts:

1. **Quick Start** — the first 15 minutes. Get your community live and ready for people.
2. **Full Guide** — a complete reference for every feature and how to use it well.

Stoke is built around one idea: **reciprocal community** — a place where every member both gives and receives. Your job as organizer isn't to perform for an audience; it's to create the conditions where members show up for each other. The tools below are there to make that easy.

---

## Part 1 — Quick Start (your first 15 minutes)

You don't need to do everything at once. A community feels alive when there's somewhere to talk, something to read, and a reason to come back. These five steps get you there.

### 1. Create your community
Go to **Discover → Create a community** (or `/communities/new`). You'll choose:

- **Name & description** — what it is and who it's for, in plain language.
- **Category** — helps people find you in the directory.
- **Join mode** — how people get in (see [Join modes](#join-modes)). If you're not sure, **Request to join** is a safe default.
- **Listed or unlisted** — listed communities appear in the public directory; unlisted ones are reachable only by direct link or invite.

When you create a community, you automatically become its **owner** and first **organizer**.

### 2. Write a welcome post
On your community page, open the **Bulletin** tab and post a short welcome. Tell people what the community is for, what they can expect, and what to do first. This is the first thing new members see — make it warm.

### 3. Create a channel
Go to **Settings → Gathering Spaces** and create your first channel (for example, *#general* or *#introductions*). Channels are real-time text chat — the ongoing conversation that keeps a community feeling lively.

### 4. Invite your first members
Go to **Settings → Invite links**, generate a link, and share it. The link works even for people who don't have a Stoke account yet — they'll be guided through signup. (More in [Invites](#invites-bringing-people-in).)

### 5. Schedule an event (optional but powerful)
On the **Events** tab, create something for people to show up to — a kickoff call, an intro session, a recurring meetup. Events give members a concrete reason to return.

> **Tip:** A built-in onboarding checklist on your community page tracks these steps (welcome post → channel → first member → event) and disappears once you've done them all.

That's it. Your community is live. The rest of this guide helps you run it well.

## Part 2 — The Full Guide

### Roles: who can do what

Stoke has two layers of roles. As an organizer you only deal with the **community** layer.

**Community roles** (set per community, in Settings → Members):

| Role | What it is | Key powers |
|---|---|---|
| **Owner** | The person who created the community | Everything an organizer can do, **plus** the exclusive power to appoint Organizers |
| **Organizer** | A community leader / co-owner-equivalent | Full moderation, change member roles (appoint Moderators), email the whole community |
| **Moderator** | A trusted enforcer | Full day-to-day moderation, but cannot change roles or email all members |
| **Member** | A standard participant | Post, comment, RSVP, ask & answer questions, chat |

**What Organizers and Moderators share** (everyday moderation):
- Approve / reject / delete bulletin posts
- Approve or reject join requests
- Ban, remove, and unban members
- Create and delete events, channels, and invite links
- Approve Q&A questions and answers, and file them into categories
- Delete inappropriate channel messages

**What only Organizers can do** (governance):
- **Change member roles** — promote a member to Moderator
- **Email all members** — send a community-wide email blast

**What only the Owner can do:**
- **Appoint Organizers** — only the owner can grant or change the Organizer role. Organizers can build a moderation team, but they can't create new co-owners.

> **How to delegate moderation:** Go to **Settings → Members**, find the member, and switch their role dropdown to **Moderator**. Their powers apply *only within your community* — community roles never cross between communities.

> There's also a **platform team** layer (Stoke staff) that exists above all communities. You don't manage that, and it doesn't change how you run your community.

### Join modes

You choose how people get into your community when you create it (and you can change it later in Settings → General):

- **Open** — anyone can join instantly and become an active member. Best for low-friction, public communities.
- **Request to join** — people request, and an organizer or moderator approves them. Best when you want a light gate without being exclusive. *(Recommended default.)*
- **Invite-only** — people can only join via an invite link, and even then they land in the approval queue. Best for private or curated groups.

> Note: invite links always route people through the approval queue for request and invite-only communities — so an invite is a door, not an automatic membership.

---

### The Bulletin board

The bulletin is your community's front page — announcements, shares, questions to the group, recommendations.

- **Members can submit posts**, but their posts **require approval** before they're visible. You'll see pending posts to approve or reject.
- **Posts from organizers and moderators publish immediately** — no approval needed.
- Posts support **photos** (single image or a gallery), which open in a lightbox when clicked.
- Approve, reject, or delete any post from the bulletin tab.

**Use it well:** Pin the tone early with your welcome post. Approve generously but keep spam and off-topic content out — the bar is "does this serve the community?"

---

### Gathering Spaces (channels)

Channels are real-time text chat — the day-to-day conversation.

- Create and manage channels in **Settings → Gathering Spaces**.
- Messages appear in real time; consecutive messages from the same person are grouped for readability.
- Members can share links (which preview automatically) and images.
- Moderators and organizers can delete messages that don't belong.

**Use it well:** Start with one or two channels (*#general*, *#introductions*). Don't over-structure early — add channels as real needs emerge. Too many empty channels make a community feel dead.

---

### Events

Events give members a reason to show up — calls, meetups, sessions.

- **Only organizers and moderators can create events.**
- Each event has a title, description, start/end time, and a location type: **online**, **in person**, or **hybrid** (with a link or address).
- Members **RSVP**: *Going*, *Maybe*, or *Can't go*. Clicking your current status again clears it.
- Past events are tucked into a collapsible section so the tab stays focused on what's coming up.
- Members who RSVP get an **email reminder** before the event starts.

**Use it well:** Recurring events are the heartbeat of many communities. Even a simple weekly check-in gives people rhythm and a reason to return.

---

### Q&A Knowledge Base

The Q&A tab is your community's durable, searchable memory. In chat, good answers scroll away and get lost. Here, they last.

- **Any member can ask a question** and **any member can answer** — but both questions and answers **require moderator approval** before they appear. This keeps the knowledge base high-signal.
- When you approve a question, you also **file it into a category** in one step.
- You define the **categories** (topics) in **Settings → Q&A categories**.
- The asker — or a moderator — can mark **one accepted answer** per question. There are no upvotes or karma, on purpose: the goal is genuine help, not points-chasing.
- Every question has its own durable, shareable link.

**Use it well:** Seed it with the questions your community already asks over and over. Ten solid entries on day one makes the value obvious. Answer a few yourself to set the tone.

---

### Members & moderation

Manage everyone in **Settings → Members**. Members are grouped into **Pending requests**, **active Members**, and **Banned**.

- **Approve / reject** join requests (for request and invite-only communities).
- **Change roles** — promote to Moderator (owner can also promote to Organizer).
- **Ban** — keeps the person out of this community (community ban; account-wide platform bans are a separate, staff-only thing).
- **Remove** — removes them from the community without banning.
- **Unban** — restores a banned member as a regular member.

Every moderation action is recorded in the **Audit log** at the bottom of Settings, so your whole team can see what happened and when.

**Use it well:** Build a small moderation team before you need one. Promote one or two trusted, active members to Moderator early — moderation is much easier shared.

---

### Invites — bringing people in

Generate invite links in **Settings → Invite links**.

- Set an optional **maximum number of uses** and an optional **expiry date** — or leave both open for an evergreen link.
- **Copy** the link to share anywhere; **revoke** it any time to shut it off.
- Invite links work for people **without a Stoke account** — they'll be prompted to sign in or sign up, then land back on your community.

**Use it well:** Use a short-lived, limited-use link for one-off recruitment pushes, and a separate evergreen link for your website or Discord.

---

### Emailing your members

Organizers can send a **community-wide email blast** from **Settings → Email members**.

- Only **organizers** (and the owner) can send blasts — moderators cannot.
- There's a rate limit to protect your members' inboxes, so use it for things that matter: a big event, an important announcement, a call for help.
- Stoke never exposes members' email addresses to you — outreach is proxied.

**Use it well:** Email is your highest-attention channel and your easiest one to overuse. Reserve it for moments that genuinely deserve everyone's attention.

### Plans & limits

Every organizer starts on the **Free** plan. You can upgrade any time in **Settings → Billing**.

| Plan | Price | Communities | Members | Channels |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Free** | $0 | 1 | up to 50 | up to 3 |
| **Starter** | $19/mo | more | more | more |
| **Pro** | $49/mo | unlimited | unlimited | unlimited |

When you hit a limit (e.g. inviting your 51st member on Free), Stoke shows an **Upgrade** prompt that links straight to billing. Nothing breaks — you're just asked to upgrade to grow further.

### Getting help

- **Support** — open a ticket from the **Support** link in your account menu. You can tag it to a community if it's community-specific.
- Stoke staff handle account issues, bug reports, and platform-level questions.

## A short philosophy of running a good community

Tools don't make a community — people do. But the right tools, used well, make it dramatically easier for people to show up for each other. A few principles:

- **Go first.** Post the welcome. Ask the first question. Answer a few. Communities take their cues from their organizer.
- **Delegate early.** Promote trusted members to Moderator before you're overwhelmed. Shared moderation is sustainable moderation.
- **Reward contribution, not consumption.** Celebrate the member who answered a question or organized a meetup. That's the behavior that compounds.
- **Keep the bar honest.** Approve generously, but protect the signal. Every post and answer that appears is an implicit endorsement.
- **Let it breathe.** Don't over-build channels and categories before there's a reason. Structure should follow life, not precede it.

Welcome to Stoke. Go build something reciprocal.
